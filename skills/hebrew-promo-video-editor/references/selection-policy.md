# Promotional selection policy

Use this policy when choosing ranges from a Hebrew interview, testimonial, podcast, or radio segment.

## First decide whether the material fits an ad

An advertising candidate needs at least one self-contained, audience-relevant claim or emotion and enough visual/audio continuity to present it honestly. Favor a speaker explaining what the project is, why it matters, what distinguishes it, or why the viewer should trust it. A polished image alone is not a persuasive message.

Classify each range before cutting:

| Material | Advertising treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Clear, specific statement about the project or viewer benefit | Candidate main spoken beat; verify factual claims. |
| Personal story or testimonial with a concrete link to the project | Candidate hook or proof, provided the link survives the cut. |
| Branded footage, logo, site imagery or clean architectural/product shot | Opening, bridge or closing visual; keep a different narrator muted unless intentionally featured. |
| Greeting, long setup, interviewer question, tangent, repeated point | Usually omit unless indispensable to understand the answer. |
| Strong visual with no matching spoken claim | B-roll only; do not fabricate a quotation or subtitle from landing-page copy. |
| Ambiguous claim, unclear audio, unsupported number or incomplete sentence | Do not use until verified; an attractive edit cannot cure factual uncertainty. |

If the user's objective is faithful full-video translation or accessibility captions, route to `hebrew-subtitle-translator` instead of scoring segments for advertising relevance.

## Build one persuasive arc

Rank candidate material by its contribution to this sequence:

1. **Hook** — a concrete promise, tension, surprising fact, or direct audience need in the first sentence.
2. **Identity** — what the project/product is and where it is, stated clearly enough for a cold viewer.
3. **Value** — a tangible differentiator, benefit, scarcity point, or use case.
4. **Trust** — verifiable experience, evidence, named partner, progress, or another credibility signal.
5. **Close** — a complete final sentence or a short branded visual/CTA.

Do not force every category into every video. Remove repeated setup, greetings, filler, interviewer prompts, tangents, and weaker duplicates. Prefer roughly 20–60 seconds unless the user specifies another target.

## Score candidate ranges

Score each candidate from 1–5 on:

- advertising relevance to the supplied project;
- clarity without outside conversation context;
- specificity and credibility;
- emotional or practical audience value;
- clean entry and exit points;
- visual usability.

Reject a high-scoring quote if it creates an unsupported claim, depends on a missing question, changes speaker unexpectedly, or cannot end cleanly.

## Editing invariants

- Keep retained beats from the same speaker in source order unless the user explicitly requests a montage that reorders them.
- Start before the first phoneme and end after the sentence cadence; include small handles, then trim by listening.
- Do not join clauses in a way that changes causality, certainty, scope, or who made the claim.
- Subtitle what was said, not rewritten landing-page copy. Use verified marketing copy only in clearly separate title/CTA graphics.
- Treat numbers and commercial terms as high risk for transcription errors. Confirm them against the audio.
- If separate promo footage contains another narrator, either keep a deliberately self-contained intro/outro or mute it and use only the visual. Do not let the narrator interrupt the main speaker accidentally.
- A closing brand frame should be intentionally designed or cleanly sourced. Do not use an arbitrary final source frame as an end card.
- Inspect a few frames before and after every intended opening and closing cut. Avoid motion blur, a transition frame or a facial expression that looks accidental at time zero or at the last frame.
- If the chosen closing phrase does not finish a thought, extend the same speaker to a complete sentence or choose another ending; do not patch it with unrelated narration.

## Candidate table

Before rendering, maintain a compact table with source timecodes, exact spoken idea, score, role in the arc, and any verification concern. When the brief is clear, choose the strongest coherent set autonomously; present alternatives when materially different messages are possible.

## Final factual pass

Check the transcript, subtitles, hook text, CTA, and visible landing-page-derived copy together. A correction to a name, number, or claim must propagate to every occurrence, not only the subtitle line where it was first noticed.
